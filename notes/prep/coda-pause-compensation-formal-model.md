This note states a cautious formal model for the timing equivalence of heavy syllables. Its purpose is not to claim that Akkadian timing has been reconstructed directly, but to make explicit the comparative logic by which closed and long-vowel syllables may converge in perceived weight (Kosuke Sugai 2017; Tomoko Kozasa 2002; Darya Kavitskaya 2001; Morley and Smith 2022).

The first point is structural. Akkadian and the wider moraic literature classify `CVC` and `CVV` as heavy, while `CVVC` is superheavy (Hayes 1995; Kager 2009; Huehnergard 2011; Greenstein 1984). The second point is phonetic. Raw segment durations do not guarantee direct equality between a long vowel and a short vowel plus measured coda. Japanese quantity-sensitive timing shows that ordinary long-vowel production may remain below the perceptual boundary that signals a second mora, while compensatory-lengthening work shows that transitional material may contribute to the effective timing of a syllable (Kosuke Sugai 2017; Tomoko Kozasa 2002; Darya Kavitskaya 2001).

For that reason, the useful formal object is not simply `CVC = O + V + C`, but `CVC = O + V + C + p`, where `p` is an effective boundary contribution. Depending on context, `p` may correspond to coda prolongation, gestural switching time, release-to-onset preparation, or a short edge interval rather than to a full lexical segment (Morley and Smith 2022; Darya Kavitskaya 2001). On this reading, the heavier structure is carried by the effective post-vocalic contribution `C + p`, not by measured coda duration alone.

PROPOSED MODEL: A practical interval version may begin with a light attractor `K1`, a heavy attractor `K2`, and a superheavy attractor `K3`, while allowing the heavy and superheavy classes to absorb boundary timing through `p` or through a word-level adjustment term. This is a heuristic modeling step, not a direct phonetic recovery.
