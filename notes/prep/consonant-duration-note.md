The consonant-duration evidence is now strong enough to support a revised article statement, though not a fully determinate consonant-by-consonant calibration. The principal gain is that the model no longer needs to contrast only closure consonants with a single undifferentiated non-closure remainder. The present comparative evidence supports a three-class baseline: closure consonants, fricatives, and sonorants/glides.

Closure consonants remain the firmest class. Their comparative basis is stronger because stop duration is repeatedly measured through a common physical cue, namely closure duration, and because both positional differences and singleton-geminate contrasts are comparatively well anchored. By contrast, the non-stop domain is now better documented but still less uniform. Fricatives are measured mainly through noise duration or onset duration, whereas sonorants and glides are often described through murmur duration, transition duration, or full segment duration. The right article-level claim is therefore not that every class is equally well measured, but that the three-class distinction is now methodologically necessary.

## Suggested prose for the manuscript

The consonantal side of the model may now be stated more precisely than before. Comparative phonetic evidence suggests that a simple closure-versus-continuant distinction is too coarse for timing purposes. Closure consonants remain the most securely anchored class because they are defined by measurable silent closure intervals and because comparative work provides both positional values and a relatively firm singleton-geminate contrast. The non-closure domain, however, is not homogeneous. Fricatives tend to occupy a heavier interval through sustained noise, whereas sonorants and glides occupy time through murmur and transition. The resulting three-class reduction does not recover Akkadian milliseconds directly, but it does provide a more defensible comparative baseline for assigning consonantal duration in a modeled realization.

At the same time, the evidential asymmetry should be stated openly. Fricative and sonorant values are not supported by the same level of normalization as stop closures. They are therefore better treated as class-level ranges used in bounded sensitivity testing rather than as exact recovered constants. This preserves methodological caution while still allowing the article to move beyond an undifferentiated non-stop category.
