This note records one methodological correction that matters for the whole timing model: stop closure duration, fricative full duration, and sonorant transition measures should not be compared as though they were the same acoustic quantity (Yungdo Yun 2022; Howell and Rosen 1983; Darya Kavitskaya 2001; Billington 2015; Gibson et al. 2013).

Stop data are the most comparable because the major studies repeatedly measure closure duration across onset, coda, intervocalic, and geminate contexts (Yungdo Yun 2022; Hankamer, Lahiri, and Koreman 1989). Fricative values are less normalized because some sources report onset duration, others relative excess over stops, and others broader segmental measures (Howell and Rosen 1983). Sonorant and glide evidence is still more heterogeneous because it mixes murmur duration, full segment duration, and transition length (Billington 2015; Gibson et al. 2013; Darya Kavitskaya 2001).

The effect of this correction is methodological, not destructive. Once the comparison is restricted to like with like, the stop data become comparatively stable. The non-stop classes remain useful, but they support evidence-ranked class bands rather than one table on the same evidential footing as stop closure.
