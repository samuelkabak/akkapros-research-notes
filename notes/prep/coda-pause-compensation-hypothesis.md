This note states the compensation hypothesis in a restrained form suitable for further source checking. The hypothesis begins from a familiar tension in quantity-sensitive timing: a long vowel is often not equal in raw milliseconds to a short vowel plus measured coda, yet `CVV` and `CVC` continue to pattern together as heavy structures (Kosuke Sugai 2017; Tomoko Kozasa 2002; Hayes 1995; Kager 2009).

Comparative work suggests that raw segment addition may not exhaust the relevant timing domain. Diphthongal and compensatory-lengthening studies indicate that transitional material can contribute to perceived duration, and recent phonetic discussion of gestural phasing suggests that apparent acoustic duration depends partly on overlap and release timing rather than on isolated segment measures alone (Darya Kavitskaya 2001; Morley and Smith 2022). This makes it plausible to ask whether a coda contributes not only its measured duration but also a following boundary interval.

PROPOSED HYPOTHESIS: The effective weight of a closed syllable may be modeled as `coda duration + boundary contribution`, where the boundary contribution is filled by coda prolongation, articulatory switching, or a short edge interval depending on context. Under that hypothesis, `CVC` and `CVV` can converge in effective timing without requiring exact arithmetic identity between long-vowel duration and short-vowel-plus-measured-coda duration.

This hypothesis remains to be verified. The present bibliography supports the plausibility of compensation and transitional contribution, but it does not yet provide a direct Akkadian measurement of the additional boundary term.
