## M-175-Oral-Acquisition-Justification

**Domain:** Article / Conclusion / Broader Implication

**WHAT:** The article's conclusion argues that computational prosody is not merely a descriptive tool but a bridge from silent philology to living speech. This claim is now supported by second-language acquisition research showing that learning a language solely through written materials is insufficient for developing spontaneous speech. Four sources from the NotebookLM source `notes/notebooklm/language-acquisition.md` are used:

1. **Uchihara et al. 2022** — Experimental comparison of input modes: learners who rely exclusively on text are judged as significantly more heavily accented and less comprehensible than those who receive auditory input. Directly relevant to the claim that text-only learning is insufficient for spoken fluency.

2. **Bassetti 2024** — Orthographic effects in L2 phonetics and phonology: written forms create a misleading visual anchor, and the brain applies native-language spelling and pronunciation rules to the target language. These errors are notoriously resistant to correction even after years of naturalistic exposure.

3. **Mairano et al. 2018** — Orthographic interference: the Finnish/double-letter example shows that Italian learners of English incorrectly geminate in "Finnish" because they see double "n"s. These orthography-induced errors persist even in bilinguals who have lived in the target-language country for years.

4. **Flege and Bohn 2021** — The Revised Speech Learning Model (SLM-r): without the constant reference of heard speech, learners cannot attune their perceptual system to the language-specific phonetic detail required for accurate production. The native language acts as a "sieve" that passes only familiar phonetic information.

5. **Piske and Young-Scholten 2009** — The auditory feedback loop: successful production must be preceded by a period of hearing the language to build correct mental targets; otherwise, the learner merely reinforces their own self-produced errors.

**WHY:** The broader implication was previously supported by weaker sources (Fatemipour and Moharamzadeh 2015, which addressed grammar acquisition rather than pronunciation; and Karimova 2024, an institutional publication). The new sources are stronger: Uchihara et al. (2022) is published in *The Modern Language Journal* (top-tier SLA journal), Bassetti (2024) is a Cambridge Handbook chapter, Flege and Bohn (2021) is the definitive statement of the SLM-r model, and Piske and Young-Scholten (2009) is an edited volume from Multilingual Matters.

**THUS:** The conclusion now reads:

> The importance of this step should not be underestimated. Research on second-language acquisition shows that learning a language solely through written materials is insufficient for developing spontaneous speech. Experimental comparisons of input modes have found that learners who rely exclusively on text are judged as significantly more heavily accented and less comprehensible than those who receive auditory input (Uchihara et al. 2022). The reason is that written forms create a misleading visual anchor: the brain applies native-language spelling and pronunciation rules to the target language, a phenomenon known as orthographic interference, and these errors are notoriously resistant to correction even after years of naturalistic exposure (Bassetti 2024; Mairano et al. 2018). Without the constant reference of heard speech, learners cannot attune their perceptual system to the language-specific phonetic detail required for accurate production (Flege and Bohn 2021), and the auditory feedback loop necessary to convert abstract phonological knowledge into physical motor routines never develops (Piske and Young-Scholten 2009).

The "author believes, intuitively" framing was removed because the claim is now empirically grounded rather than merely intuitive.

## M-176-Argument-Structure

**Domain:** Article / Conclusion / Argumentation

**WHAT:** The broader implication argument now proceeds in four steps:

1. **Claim:** The methodology extends beyond Akkadian to any dead language with a well-described stress system (Latin, Koine Greek, Old Egyptian).

2. **Evidence from SLA:** Five acquisition sources show that text-only learning is insufficient — input mode effects (Uchihara et al. 2022), orthographic interference (Bassetti 2024; Mairano et al. 2018), perceptual attunement (Flege and Bohn 2021), and the auditory feedback loop (Piske and Young-Scholten 2009).

3. **Application to dead languages:** If spoken fluency is the goal (pedagogical performance, public engagement, embodied understanding), a computational prosody model is a prerequisite, not a luxury.

4. **Conclusion:** Computational prosody is a bridge from silent philology to living speech.

**WHY:** This structure moves from the general methodological claim, through empirical evidence from a related domain, to a specific application, and finally to a normative conclusion. It avoids the weakness of relying on intuition alone.

**THUS:** The argument is now empirically grounded rather than merely intuitive.

## M-179-nPVI-V-Manual-Verification

**Domain:** Article / Metrics / nPVI-V / Verification

**WHAT:** A manual computation of nPVI-V was performed on the first 200 data lines of `outputs/full-corpus/corpus-lob_phone.txt` (the accentuated stream) to verify the full-corpus value and understand why it is low.

**Method:**
- Extracted vowel rows (category = V) from the first 200 phone rows after the YAML header
- Read the duration field (9th pipe-delimited field, zero-padded 4 chars)
- Computed nPVI-V = 100 × [Σ |dᵢ - dᵢ₊₁| / ((dᵢ + dᵢ₊₁)/2)] / (n-1)

**Results:**
- 78 vowel intervals found
- nPVI-V = 26.40 (vs. full-corpus value of 28.98)
- rPVI-V = 45.35 ms
- Mean vowel duration = 133.68 ms

**Vowel duration distribution:**
- 58 of 78 intervals = exactly 110 ms (short-vowel default)
- 20 intervals = 131–280 ms (long vowels: 131, 140, 160, 188, 200, 211, 212, 232, 260, 280)

**Why nPVI-V is low:**
The nPVI-V measures the normalized variability of *successive* vocalic intervals. Because most successive pairs are 110→110 (ratio = 0), the occasional 110→280 or 280→110 transitions are diluted across the many identical pairs. This is a direct consequence of Akkadian's lack of vowel reduction: short vowels are all ~110 ms regardless of stress, unlike English where unstressed vowels reduce to schwa (~30–50 ms), creating large durational contrasts between successive vowels.

**Implication for the article:**
The nPVI-V does not point away from stress-timing. It points to a specific phonological property — the absence of vowel reduction — that is independently known to be true of Akkadian. The direction-of-movement argument (nPVI-V rises from 22.72 to 28.98 after realization) is more informative than the absolute value.

**THUS:** The article's Section 4.4 was expanded with this analysis to address the reviewer's concern that the nPVI-V anomaly was treated too lightly.

## M-180-Review-Response-nPVI-V

**Domain:** Article / Review Response / Metrics

**WHAT:** The reviewer's concern about nPVI-V (from `local-only/reviews/review-v10-jcs.md`) was:

> "The nPVI-V values are anomalous. Akkadian's nPVI-V of 28.98 is far below the stress-timed range (English 73, Dutch 82) and even below syllable-timed Spanish (36). The article explains this by noting that Akkadian lacked vowel reduction, which is a reasonable phonological argument. But the gap is so large (28.98 vs. 73 for English) that it raises a question: does the nPVI-V actually support the stress-timed reading, or does it point in a different direction? The article should address this more directly rather than treating it as a minor deviation."

**Response:** The manual computation (M-179) confirms the value and reveals the mechanism: the nPVI-V is low because most successive vowel pairs are identical (110→110), not because the long vowels are short. The absence of vowel reduction means short vowels have a fixed duration, eliminating the durational contrast that drives nPVI-V up in English. The direction-of-movement argument (22.72 → 28.98) shows that the realization layer *does* increase vocalic variability, even if the baseline is lower than in languages with vowel reduction.

**THUS:** The article now includes a detailed explanation of the nPVI-V distribution, grounded in direct data inspection, rather than treating it as a minor deviation.
