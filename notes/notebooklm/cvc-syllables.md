### **American English CVC Durations**
Data from the Buckeye Corpus, which tracks natural conversational speech, shows durations significantly lower than laboratory "target" values:
*   **Vowel Component:** The **median vowel duration** for all English CVC words in conversation is (Morley and Smith 2022) **(83)** ms.
*   **Voiced Context:** For CVC words ending in voiced obstruents, the median vowel duration drops to (Morley and Smith 2022) **(75)** ms.
*   **Coda Component:** The **median closure duration** for word-final voiceless stops (the final "C" in CVC) is (Morley and Smith 2022) **(46)** ms. 
*   **Rarity of Long Segments:** In natural speech, only (Morley and Smith 2022) **(9)**% of CVC-final voiceless stops reach a duration of **(100)** ms or above.

### **Mandarin Chinese CVC-Equivalent Durations**
Mandarin syllables take the form (C)(G)V(X), where "X" can be a coda consonant [n, ŋ], creating a CVC structure.
*   **Total Monosyllable:** The **mean duration** of the total monosyllabic stimuli used in acceptability judgment trials was (Yang Liu 2022) **(545)** ms.
*   **Natural Speech Average:** In connected speech, the average duration per syllable for Mandarin is approximately (Pellegrino et al. 2011) **(193)** ms.

### **Japanese CVC Perception and Production**
In Japanese, the closed monosyllabic word **/tan/** is a prototypical CVC structure.
*   **Perceptual Threshold:** The **durational boundary** for the first part of the syllable (/ta/)—the point at which listeners shift from perceiving it as one mora to two morae—peaks at (Sugai 2017) **(250)** ms. 
*   **Bimoraic Confidence:** In the CVC context, almost no one-mora responses were recorded once the initial /ta/ duration exceeded (Sugai 2017) **(310)** ms.

### **Spanish CVC Components**
