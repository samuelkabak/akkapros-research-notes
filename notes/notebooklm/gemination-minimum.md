Based on the empirical studies provided in the sources, the minimum duration for perceiving gemination varies significantly depending on the acoustic class of the consonant and the specific cues (closure duration vs. transition time) used by the listener.

### **1. Stops (Closure Consonants)**
For voiceless stops, the "silent interval" of the closure is the overriding perceptual cue.
*   **Geminate Threshold:** Perceptual studies on Turkish and Bengali indicate that intervals shorter than (J. Hankamer et al.) (373) **100 ms** are consistently heard as singletons, while intervals longer than (J. Hankamer et al.) (373) **180 ms** are always identified as geminates.
*   **The Ambiguous Zone:** Crossover points in identification tasks generally fall between (J. Hankamer et al.) (352, 378) **120 ms and 160 ms**, where secondary cues (like vowel duration or pitch) may start to influence the listener's judgment.

### **2. Fricatives (Continuous Noise)**
Fricatives lack a silent closure, so the perception of gemination relies on the duration of the friction noise or the interval required to "break" the sound into two units.
*   **Moraic Boundary:** For "chocked sounds" (the first half of a geminate), perceptual boundaries were found to be between (Fujisaki and Sugito 1975 cited in Kosuke Sugai 2017) (325) **152 ms and 166 ms**.
*   **Perceptual Fusion:** To distinguish a geminate from a single occurrence in a sequence, a silent interval or duration increase of approximately (Repp 1980 cited in Stephen Grossberg) (424) **150 ms longer** is required compared to the duration needed to perceive two different consonants in a cluster.

### **3. Nasals and Liquids (Continuous Frequency)**
These sonorants are categorized as "special morae" in quantity-sensitive systems like Japanese, where they function similarly to geminate stops.
*   **Syllabic Nasal Threshold:** In Japanese, the perceptual boundary for identifying an independent moraic nasal (which is effectively a geminated or "held" nasal segment) is roughly (Fujisaki and Sugito 1975 cited in Kosuke Sugai 2017) (325) **152 ms**.
*   **Production Baselines:** At regular speaking rates, the predicted duration for the first part of a geminate (C1), which includes moraic nasals, starts at (Haiping Jia et al.) (347) **58 ms** for fast speech and reaches (Haiping Jia et al.) (347) **158 ms** for slow speech.

### **4. Glides (Approximants)**
Glides are unique because they are defined by the **duration of their formant transitions** rather than a steady-state duration.
*   **The Glide "Floor":** Transitions (LINGUISTICS 401) (322) **less than 40 ms** are perceived as stops (like [b] or [d]) rather than glides.
*   **The Glide "Ceiling":** Transitions (LINGUISTICS 401) (322) **longer than 100 ms** are no longer perceived as glides but as a sequence of two vowels (a diphthong).
*   **Akkadian Protraction:** Since glides in Akkadian were likely realized as a "holding of the articulation," they follow the (Giorgio Buccellati 1996) (709) **"protracted articulation"** principle, though the sources do not provide an exact minimum ms threshold for their gemination beyond these transition bounds.
