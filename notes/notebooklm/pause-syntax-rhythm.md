### 1\. Categorization of Pause Types

Research categorizes pauses primarily by their **syntactic location**, which determines their duration and function:

* **Short (In-Clause) Pauses:** These occur in the middle of a subject-and-predicate unit (Anjarningsih 2024\) 1\. They are often associated with **hesitation** or **word-finding** difficulties (Anjarningsih 2024; Best 2005\) 2, 3\.  
* **Long (Clause-Boundary) Pauses:** These are placed between the last word of a clause and the first word of the subsequent clause (Anjarningsih 2024; Tavakoli 2011\) 1, 4\. They are associated with **thought organization**, information structuring, and speech planning (Hawkins 1971; Tavakoli 2011\) 5, 6\.

### 3\. The 600 ms "Optimum" vs. Functional Reality

Having **600 ms** for every comma does not "sum up" in natural speech because the duration signals the **strength of the boundary** to the listener:

* **The Threshold of Perception:** A silent interval is only categorized as a "pause" if it lasts for (Goldman-Eisler cited in Anjarningsih 2024\) **250 ms** or more; shorter gaps are often just breathing patterns (Goldman-Eisler; Stanislawski 2021\) 9, 10\.  
* **Planning Complexity:** Clause-boundary pauses are naturally longer because the speaker must make decisions regarding "content, syntactic structure, and information distribution" (Hawkins 1971 cited in Anjarningsih 2024\) 6\.  
* **Naturalness for Synthesis:** While a fixed (Lin 2021\) **600 ms** (0.6 s) 8, 11 is heard as having the highest naturalness in controlled experiments for Mandarin and English, actual spontaneous speech production involves much more **variability** to prevent the rhythm from sounding "mechanical" or "rushed" (Lin 2021\) 12, 13\.  
* **Listener Tolerance:** Pauses exceeding (Kendrick and Torreira 2015\) **700 ms** 14 in conversational response slots can be perceived negatively, but in continuous narrative speech, longer pauses at major boundaries are expected and aid **comprehensibility** (Bae 2015; Lin 2021\) 8, 15\.
