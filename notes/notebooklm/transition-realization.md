The effective realization of a vowel transition as a weak **/j/** or **/w/** depends on the **acoustic similarity** between the glide and its surrounding vowels, as well as phonological rules for **hiatus resolution** and **allophonic harmony**.

### **Acoustic Detectability and Vowel Quality**
The identifiability of a transition as a glide is determined by the formant targets (F2 and F3) of the neighboring vowels:
*   **High/Front Contexts:** For high front vowels like [i, e] or high vowels like [u], a transition into a /j/ glide is **not easily detectable** because these vowels already have relatively high F2 and F3 values that mimic the glide's target (Rebeka Campos-Astorkiza 2005) **(17)**. Under these conditions, the glide is often reanalyzed as simple vowel lengthening (Rebeka Campos-Astorkiza 2005) **(17)**.
*   **Low/Back Contexts:** Transitions from low or back vowels like [a, o] into /j/ are **easy to identify** because these vowels have lower F2 and F3 values, making the articulatory movement toward the glide target distinct (Rebeka Campos-Astorkiza 2005) **(17)**.

### **Conditions for /j/ and /w/ Realization**
Vowel transitions are realized through specific phonological environments and articulatory constraints:
*   **Hiatus Avoidance:** In languages like Mushuau Innu, weak glides are realized to resolve vowel hiatus. Under this condition, /i/ and /u/ are converted into glides **/j/** and **/w/** when positioned next to other vowels (Erica Woolridge 2015) **(816)**. This can involve a long vowel [i:] gliding into the following syllable (Erica Woolridge 2015) **(821)**.
*   **Epenthesis:** In some sequences, such as between [i] and [u], a weak **epenthetic glide** is inserted to maintain the syllable boundary (Erica Woolridge 2015) **(821)**.
*   **Allophonic Harmony (Mandarin):** The schwa vowel /ə/ is realized as the mid round back vowel [o] when preceded or followed by **/w/** or [u] due to **roundness and backness harmony** (Yang Liu 2022) **(400, 401)**. Conversely, if the schwa is preceded by high front glides like [j], it is fronted to [e] (Yang Liu 2022) **(400, 401)**.
*   **Intrinsic Transition Duration:** The "light glide" element of a transition in Mandarin—the articulatory movement from an onset into a vowel—is extremely brief, measuring approximately (Yang Liu 2022) **(468)** **11** ms.

### **Secondary Articulation vs. Segmental Glide**
The "weakness" of the transition is often a result of its status as a secondary articulation:
*   **Palatalization Overlap:** In Mandarin, the pre-vocalic glide [j] is typically realized as **palatalization of the preceding consonant** (Cj) rather than a separate segmental glide. This is because it lacks a "prolonged steady state" and shows heavy articulatory overlap with the initial consonant (Yang Liu 2022) **(590, 599)**.
*   **Vowel-to-Glide Coarticulation:** Realizations of the glide are "similar" to Russian palatalized glides, where the vowel-to-glide coarticulation is smaller than in true segmental glides (Yang Liu 2022) **(587, 589)**.
*   **Manner Effects:** The duration of the transition is further conditioned by the following segment; transitions into liquids are (Rebeka Campos-Astorkiza 2005) **(13) relatively long**, whereas transitions into trills and taps are (Rebeka Campos-Astorkiza 2005) **(13) short**.
