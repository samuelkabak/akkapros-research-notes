In both Akkadian and Syrian Arabic, the first syllable of a word or phrase serves as a critical structural unit for accentuation, functioning as the default landing site for stress and often bearing initial intonational prominence (John Huehnergard 2011) (45); (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (7). While phonetic weight is technically concentrated in the vocalic nucleus, the "whole" syllable prominence is achieved through a coordinated articulation of the onset consonant, the vowel, and accompanying pitch movements (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (99, 116).

### **1. The First Syllable as a Default Landing Site**
In standard descriptions of Semitic stress systems, the first syllable acts as the automatic position for prominence when no heavy syllables are present to attract stress elsewhere (John Huehnergard 2011) (676).
*   **Akkadian Rule:** If a word contains only light syllables (sequences of CV), the stress defaults to the **first syllable** (John Huehnergard 2011) (676). Examples include words like *zikarum* ('man') or *šunu* ('they'), where the initial CV syllable receives the word-level accent (John Huehnergard 2011) (676).
*   **Syrian Arabic Pattern:** Similar behavior is observed in Syrian Arabic words such as *kátab* ('he wrote'), where the stress falls on the **initial syllable** (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (105). This initial prominence helps listeners delineate word boundaries in continuous speech (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (112, 115).

### **2. Phonetic Realization: Consonant + Vowel + Intonation**
The accentuation of the first syllable as a "whole" involves distinct acoustic roles for its constituent parts:
*   **The Onset Consonant (Timing):** In moraic theory, onset consonants are considered **weightless** (non-moraic) and are associated with the following mora rather than carrying their own independent weight (Darya Kavitskaya 2001) (228). However, they serve as the **alignment point** for pitch accents; in Syrian Arabic, the low-tone part of a pitch accent ($L^*$) is specifically aligned with both the **onset and the nucleus** of the accented syllable (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (126).
*   **The Vocalic Nucleus (Duration/Pitch):** The vowel within the first syllable is the primary carrier of the stress cues (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (103). Stressed syllables are typically characterized by **higher pitch levels, longer duration, and greater intensity** (René Kager 2009) (401). In your Akkadian prosody model, if the first syllable is targeted for prominence, its moraic count is adjusted through vowel lengthening to satisfy the rhythmic budget (research-notes.md) (812, 822).
*   **Intonation (Pitch Contours):** In **declarative sentences** in Syrian Arabic, there is a characteristic **initial pitch rise** on the first accented syllable (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (99, 115). This pitch rise does not necessarily form a sharp peak; instead, the maximum pitch often **stretches over the entire stressed syllable**, sometimes creating a "plateau" or "hat pattern" (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (116).

### **3. Intonational Phrasing and Phrasal Onsets**
Accentuation of the first syllable also plays a demarcative role at the level of the phrase:
*   **Initial Pitch Rise:** Declarative tunes frequently begin with a pitch rise on the first syllable of the first word to signal the beginning of a new information unit (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (99, 115).
*   **Pragmatic Prominence:** In questions or focused utterances, the first syllable may receive an **expanded pitch range** or an exaggerated pre-pausal drawl to signal sentence mode (Malek Al Hasan 2022) (99, 121). 
*   **Restoration Rules:** In your computational algorithm, word-boundary interactions (sandhi) and the restoration of glottal stops at the beginning of vowel-initial words ensure that the first syllable always has an audible onset to serve as the start of this prominent interval (research-notes.md) (821); (John Huehnergard 2011) (671).
