The distinction between a physical silence and a perceived interruption of speech is defined by specific temporal thresholds, as the human brain categorizes very brief silences as articulatory features rather than actual pauses.

### **Perceptual and Linguistic Thresholds**
*   **The Standard Perceptual Threshold:** Humans typically perceive a silence as an intentional "pause" or interruption only when its duration reaches (Brigitte Zellner 1994) **(475, 617, 678, 739) 200 to 250 ms**. 
*   **The Linguistic Classification Threshold:** In many studies, a segment of silence is formally classified as a speech pause once it exceeds (I-Ping Wan et al. 2024/2025) **(222, 233) 150 ms**.
*   **The Functional Intentionality Threshold:** Some researchers argue for a lower limit, suggesting that silences as short as (Adolf E. Hieke et al. 1983) **(377, 425) 130 ms** are already psychologically functional and intentional.
*   **Acoustic Detection Baseline:** For the purpose of automatic data processing, researchers often use a minimum cutoff of (Sofia Gustafson-Čapková and Beáta Megyesi 2001) **(14, 16) 100 ms** to identify acoustic correlates of pausing.

### **Non-Interruptive Silences (Articulatory Breaks)**
Silences below these thresholds are usually not perceived as interruptions because they are required for speech production:
*   **Intra-segmental Silences:** Brief silences related to vocal tract occlusions, such as the (Brigitte Zellner 1994) **(469, 611, 673, 734) 96 ms** gap in the word "happy," are processed as part of the consonant (VOT) rather than an interruption of flow.
*   **Rhythmic Entrainment Gaps:** The brain can maintain rhythmic synchronization during brief gaps, but entrainment begins to drop significantly when anisochrony reaches (Diana Cutanda et al. 2019) **(1) 100 ms to 200 ms**.

### **Contextual Perception**
*   **Cognitive Load:** The perception of a silence as an interruption is also tied to information density; hesitation pauses are often located at points of high information content where the "speech has raced ahead of cognitive activity," requiring a break of at least (Brigitte Zellner 1994) **(483, 625, 685, 746) 200 ms** for the planning process to catch up,.
*   **Genre Sensitivity:** In poetry reciting, the proportion of short pauses (defined as (Pavel Šturm and Jan Volín 2023) **(365, 382) 130 to 250 ms**) is significantly higher than in political or news speech, as they are used to mark line endings and maintain the aesthetic rhythm without a full stop in the speech flow.
