Based on the empirical studies provided in the sources, the classification of speech segments—consonants, vowels, and pauses—is defined by specific numeric ranges in milliseconds (ms). These values vary according to phonetic type, phonological quantity (length), and structural position.

### 1\. Classification of Consonants by Duration and Position

Consonants exhibit significant durational variation based on their manner of articulation, their position within a word, and whether they are phonologically single or geminated.

* **By Manner of Articulation:**  
* **Stops (Plosives):** These are defined by a complete closure. In English, word-initial voiceless stops like **/p/** have a closure duration of approximately **120 ms**, while voiced stops like **/b/** are shorter at approximately **75 ms** (Lisker 1957 cited in Yun 2022\) 1, 2\.  
* **Affricates and Fricatives:** These are intrinsically longer than stops because they involve a frication phase (Grossberg) 3, 4\. In running speech, word-initial voiceless fricatives like **/ʃ/** (**171 ms**) are significantly longer than affricates like **/tʃ/** (**85 ms**) (Howell & Rosen 1983 cited in Grossberg) 3, 5\.  
* **By Position (Onset vs. Coda vs. Intervocalic):**  
* Empirical studies on Korean stops show a hierarchy where durations are longest **between vowels (intervocalic)** (**113 ms**), followed by **word-initial (onset)** (**108 ms**), and are shortest at the **word-final (coda)** position (**103 ms**) (Yun 2022\) 6, 7\.  
* **Place of Articulation:** Within these positions, the specific place of articulation creates a durational hierarchy: **Bilabial (114 ms)** \> **Alveolar (108 ms)** \> **Velar (102 ms)** (Yun 2022\) 7, 8\.  
* **By Quantity (Gemination):**  
* Geminated (long) consonants are significantly longer than singletons. In Turkish and Bengali, singleton stops average **\~67–70 ms**, whereas geminate stops average **\~187–202 ms**, representing a ratio of approximately **1:2.5** (Hankamer et al.) 9, 10\.  
* Perceptually, listeners identify intervals shorter than **100 ms** as singletons and intervals longer than **180 ms** as geminates (Hankamer et al.) 11, 12\.

### 2\. Classification of Vowels by Duration and Position

Vowels are highly sensitive to phonological quantity and their position within the prosodic structure.

* **By Phonological Quantity (Moraic Length):**  
* In Japanese, a clear mental threshold exists between one and two morae (short vs. long vowels) at approximately **250 ms** (Sugai 2017\) 13-15.  
* In production, short vowels in fast speech average **\~60–68 ms**, while long vowels in slow speech can reach **\~168–202 ms** (Kozasa 2002; Sugai 2017\) 16-18.  
* **By Position (Final Lengthening):**  
* Vowels and syllables in the **final position** of words or phrases are significantly longer than those in initial or medial positions due to **phrase-final lengthening** (Warner & Arai 2001 cited in Kozasa 2002\) 19, 20\.  
* **By Contextual Compensation:**  
* Within a mora, vowels adjust based on the onset: a **longer onset consonant is balanced by a shorter following vowel** to maintain isochronicity (Campbell & Sagisaka 1991 cited in Kozasa 2002\) 21, 22\.

### 3\. Classification of Silent Pauses

Research distinguishes between short and long pauses based on their syntactic location and perceived naturalness.

* **Pause Threshold:** A silent interval is generally categorized as a functional "pause" only if it lasts **250 ms or more**; shorter intervals are often indicative of normal breathing or articulatory gaps (Goldman-Eisler cited in Anjarningsih 2024; Anjarningsih 2024\) 23-26.  
* **Naturalness Optimum:** To sound natural, pauses at commas and periods are often fixed at approximately **600 ms (0.6 s)** (Lin 2021\) 27, 28\.  
* **Functional Types:** In spontaneous English speech, **in-clause (short) pauses** average **0.68 s (680 ms)**, while **clause-boundary (long) pauses** average **1.78 s (1780 ms)** (Anjarningsih 2024\) 29, 30\.
