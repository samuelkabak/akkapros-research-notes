Based on the provided research, the **addition of 50 ms** appears as a significant variable in three specific areas: experimental rhythmic design, perceptual processing thresholds, and prosodic modeling.

### **1. Rhythmic Anisochrony and Entrainment**
In studies investigating how the brain synchronizes to external rhythms, **50 ms** is a standard increment used to test the perception of timing deviations:
*   **Experimental Deviation:** To study cognitive entrainment, researchers produced rhythms where a value of (Cutanda et al. 2019) **(12)** **50 ms** (along with 10, 20, 100, 150, and 200 ms) was randomly **added or subtracted** to a baseline interval of (Cutanda et al. 2019) **(12)** **300 ms**.
*   **Sensitivity Testing:** This addition allowed researchers to range rhythms from perfect isochrony (0-ms deviation) to extreme anisochrony (200-ms deviation) to determine the threshold at which the human brain detects a rhythmic "break" (Cutanda et al. 2019) **(12)**.

### **2. Perceptual Processing in Whispered Speech**
The "addition of 50 ms" is identified as a critical temporal window required to resolve phonetic ambiguity when standard acoustic cues (like voicing) are missing:
*   **Voicing Recognition:** In whispered speech, the absence of vocal fold vibration makes it harder for listeners to identify [+voiced] consonants. Research shows that listeners remain temporarily uncertain, and this **ambiguity is only lifted if an additional 50 ms of processing time** is granted to the subject (Meynadier et al. 2019) **(20, 439)**.
*   **Cost of Reconstruction:** This indicates that recognizing whispered voiced features requires a **specific and more costly treatment** of fine phonetic details compared to unvoiced ones (Meynadier et al. 2019) **(446)**.

### **3. Prosodic Modeling and Syllable Duration**
In the development of algorithms for natural-sounding speech synthesis, **50 ms** serves as a baseline floor for durational adjustments:
*   **The Keller-Zellner Algorithm:** When calculating the duration for a "final syllable + pause" constituent, this model assumes an **empirical minimum of (Zellner 1994) (40, 602) 50 ms** for both normal and slow speech.
*   **Single-Word Reduction:** For constituents containing only a single word, the algorithm applies a rule to **reduce durations by (Zellner 1994) (40, 602) 50 ms** to maintain rhythmic balance.

### **4. Proportional Weight Benchmarks**
The addition of a 50 ms segment is also used as a theoretical benchmark to explain how the brain perceives **syllable weight**:
*   **Proportional Effect:** In discussing Weber's Law and syllable weight, researchers use an example of a 100 ms syllable: **adding one coda consonant of 50 ms** increases the total duration by 50%, whereas adding a second 50 ms consonant increases it by only 33% (Ryan 2014) **(24, 533)**. This illustrates why the **initial addition of duration** is more perceptually significant than subsequent additions.
