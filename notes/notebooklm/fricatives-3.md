### **2. Mandarin Fricative Inventories and Constraints**
**Yang Liu (2022)** provide multiple tables detailing the behavior of fricatives in Mandarin Chinese:
*   **Table (1):** Displays the full consonant inventory, including six fricatives: labio-dental [f], dental [s], retroflex [ʂ, ʐ], palatal [ç], and velar [x] (370, 371).
*   **Table (16):** A detailed chart of the **Agree[back]: C[-son, +cont]j** constraint. it lists specific backness agreement violations for fricatives [f, s, ʂ, ʐ, x] and affricates when followed by the glide [j] (427).
*   **Table (34):** Lists the calculated "weights" for phonotactic constraints. The constraint requiring backness agreement for fricatives and affricates before [j] is assigned a high weight of (Liu 2022) **(6.93)** (453).

### **3. Consonant Duration Modeling (English)**
**Morley and Smith (2022)** provide statistical tables modeling the intrinsic duration of fricatives in American English:
*   **Table 5:** A mixed-effects linear regression model of consonant duration. It calculates that the **fricative manner of articulation** adds an estimated (Morley and Smith 2022) **(28.83)** ms to a segment's total duration compared to stop consonants (168).
*   **Appendix A:** Provides exhaustive word lists for **CV Fricatives** used in their research, categorized by voiced (76 words) and voiceless (40 words) codas (176, 178).

### **4. Comparative Rhythmic and Syllabic Data**
*   **Hindi Fricatives:** **Broselow et al. (1997)** in **Table I** provide mean durations for Hindi syllable rhymes. While the data is pooled for several segments [p, t, s, l], the mean durations for these codas (including the fricative [s]) range from (Broselow et al. 1997) **(98.8)** ms to **(115.6)** ms (823).
*   **English Onset Durations:** **Kevin M. Ryan (2014)** in **Figure 6** plots the mean duration of various English onsets in milliseconds. ### **5. Lopit Consonant Contrasts**
**Rosey Billington (2015)** notes in her phonological description of the Lopit language that length contrasts are proposed for a variety of consonants. In section 1.2, she lists a set of geminates including **/t:, d:, n:, l:, r, w:, j:/** and their singleton counterparts (895). Note that in this system, the vibrant **/r/** functions as the long/geminate counterpart to a singleton sonorant (895).
