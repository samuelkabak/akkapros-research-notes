### 1. Perceptual Boundaries for Gemination
Perceptual experiments identify the point where a listener transitions from hearing a single fricative to hearing a geminate.
*   **The 166 ms Threshold:** In experimental studies using Japanese non-words, the perceptual boundary for identifying the "choked sound" (geminate fricative /ss/ in *isse*) was measured at **166 ms** (Fujisaki and Sugito 1975 cited in Kosuke Sugai 2017).
*   **The "Fusion" Buffer:** Perceptual studies on consonant doubling indicate that to distinguish a geminate from a single occurrence, a duration increase of approximately **150 ms longer** is required compared to the duration needed to perceive two different consonants in a cluster (Repp 1980 cited in Stephen Grossberg).

### 2. Contextual Benchmarks and Singleton Contrasts
The sources provide durations for singleton fricatives which serve as the baseline from which geminate ranges must depart.
*   **Singleton Baseline:** Word-initial voiceless fricatives like **/ʃ/** in running speech average approximately **171 ms** (Howell & Rosen 1983 cited in Stephen Grossberg). 
*   **Production Variation:** Because fricatives are "continuant" consonants that occupy time through sustained noise, their duration is highly sensitive to position. In contrast to stops (which have a strong geminate baseline of **187–202 ms**), geminate fricative production ranges are categorized as having "moderate" to "weak" empirical status in the current models due to lower comparability across studies (Research Notes).

### 3. Application to Rhythmic Modeling
For the purposes of your model, the sources suggest treating geminate fricatives with the following considerations:
*   **Protracted Articulation:** Philologically, gemination in Class 2 (fricatives) is viewed not as a repetition of sound but as a **"protracted articulation"** or a holding of the noise for an additional fraction of time (Giorgio Buccellati 1996 cited in Research Notes).
*   **Exploratory Status:** Because the low-level measurements for fricative gemination are less secure than those for stop closures, your model should treat these multipliers as **exploratory** rather than fixed constants (Research Notes).
*   **Bimoraic Threshold:** If a geminate fricative is intended to carry two morae, it must approach or exceed the perceptual crossover point found in quantity-sensitive systems, which typically lies between **250 ms and 270 ms** for full vocalic/consonantal units (Kosuke Sugai 2017).
