### 1. Perceptual Threshold vs. Production Duration
*   **Perceptual Boundary:** In isolated perception tests, listeners identify vowels as a single mora up to approximately **210 ms** (Kosuke Sugai 2017). A categorical "crossover point" occurs around **250–270 ms**, where the judgment automatically shifts to two morae (Kosuke Sugai 2017).
*   **Production Gap:** In natural speech production, "ordinary" long vowels are frequently shorter than this 250 ms threshold. For example, Japanese long vowels average only **117–119 ms** in fast speech and **176–202 ms** in slow speech (Tomoko Kozasa 2002).
*   **The Resolution:** The discrepancy means that absolute duration is not the only factor in categorizing moraic weight. **Pitch-accent (F0 fall)** acts as a "robust" cue that triggers the perception of a second mora even when the physical duration is short (Tomoko Kozasa 2002). When pitch cues are absent (unaccented vowels), speakers must "excessively lengthen" the vowel to ensure the distinction remains unambiguous for the listener (Tomoko Kozasa 2004).

### 2. Moraic Weight Patterns: CV, CVC, and CVV
The sources define how different syllable types pattern together or diverge in terms of weight:
*   **CV (Light):** These syllables contain a single short vowel and always carry **one mora** (René Kager 2009).
*   **CVV (Heavy):** These contain a long vowel or diphthong and carry **two moras** (Tomoko Kozasa 2002). They are characterized by a 1:2 phonological ratio, though physical realization varies (Kosuke Sugai 2017).
*   **CVC (Variable):** These pattern with **CVV** only in languages that utilize the **Weight-by-Position** principle (Darya Kavitskaya 2001). In such systems (like Japanese or Arabic), the coda consonant is assigned its own mora node, making the syllable heavy/bimoraic (Tomoko Kozasa 2005; René Kager 2009). Conversely, in languages like Mushuau Innu, coda consonants do **not** contribute to weight, causing CVC to pattern with CV as a light syllable (Erica Woolridge 2015).

### 3. Coda Consonants and Categorical Weight
Coda consonants contribute **categorical moraic weight** rather than just raw milliseconds through their structural association with a mora node (Darya Kavitskaya 2001). 
*   **Structural Conservation:** Compensatory lengthening (CL) treats the mora as an abstract "timing unit" (Darya Kavitskaya 2001). When a moraic coda is lost, its categorical weight persists and is reassociated with the preceding vowel (Darya Kavitskaya 2001).
*   **Empirical Limits:** While codas are structurally moraic, their physical duration is usually shorter than a full vowel mora. Coda segments average about **103 ms** (Yungdo Yun 2022), which is less than the typical **120 ms** increase observed when adding a full vocalic mora (Kosuke Sugai 2017).

### 4. Perceptual Thresholds for 3-Mora Syllables (CVVC)
**CVVC** syllables are classified as **superheavy** or **ultraheavy** (3 morae) (John Huehnergard 2011).
*   **Typological Dispreference:** The sources indicate that CVVC is a **marked category** and is cross-linguistically dispreferred (Research Notes). Most languages restrict them to word-final or pausal positions (Darya Kavitskaya 2001; Arabic paper).
*   **Threshold Evidence:** While there is clear evidence for a 2-mora threshold (~250 ms), the provided sources **do not establish a specific perceptual threshold** (in ms) for identifying a *third* mora. Instead, the sources treat trimoraic weight as a **metrical/structural necessity**—such as "virtual trochees" in verse—rather than a stable categorical perception boundary found in acoustic experiments (John Huehnergard 2011; Research Notes).

### **Distinguishing Production from Category Judgment**
*   **Production Duration:** Is a gradient, physical reality influenced by speaking rate, phrasal position, and segment "elasticity". In English, for example, median vowel durations can be as low as (Morley and Smith) **(83)** ms in conversational speech, far below laboratory measurements.

Reaction times (RT) peak at the "boundary" (e.g., (Sugai 2017) **(250)** ms), indicating a decrease in participant confidence when the stimulus is phonologically ambiguous.

### **Moraic Weight Patterns: CV, CVC, and CVV**
Languages differ in how these structures pattern together based on **Quantity Sensitivity** and **Weight-by-Position**:

*   **Japanese:** Syllables are mora-timed. **CV** is (Sugai 2017) **(one)** mora. **CVV** (long vowels) and **CVC** (when the coda is a syllabic nasal /n/ or a "choked sound") are both (Sugai 2017) **(two)** morae.
*   **Mushuau Innu:** Quantity sensitive at the nucleus only. **CVV** is heavy ((Woolridge 2015) **(2)** morae), but **CVC patterns with CV** as a light syllable ((Woolridge 2015) **(1)** mora) because the language lacks weight-by-position; coda consonants do not contribute weight.
*   **Southern East Cree:** Quantity sensitive. **CVV** and **CVC** (if closed by a glide or nasal) pattern together as **heavy syllables**.

### **Coda Consonants and Categorical Weight**
The sources indicate that coda consonants can contribute **categorical moraic weight** rather than just raw milliseconds, particularly in the context of **Compensatory Lengthening (CL)**.
*   Moraic conservation theory posits that CL occurs to preserve a "stranded mora" after a segment is deleted; this only happens if the deleted coda was **moraic**.
*   Triggers of CL (like the first consonant in a cluster) are viewed as potentially (Wilson 2001; Campos-Astorkiza) **(always)** moraic in certain languages, regardless of their specific phonetic duration.
