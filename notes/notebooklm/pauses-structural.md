### **1. Structural Hierarchy and Mandatory Nature**
In articulate read speech, the decision to pause is governed by **overt text structure**, which acts as a strong constraint on the reader (Pavel Šturm and Jan Volín 2023) **(349)**. 
*   **The 100% Rule:** At the end of a full sentence (period), a physical pause is produced (Pavel Šturm and Jan Volín 2023) **(318, 348) 100%** of the time. Similarly, at the end of a larger unit like a stanza (paragraph equivalent), a pause is also realized (Pavel Šturm and Jan Volín 2023) **(318) 100%** of the time.
*   **Internal Punctuation:** At commas or internal punctuation, the probability of a pause is (Pavel Šturm and Jan Volín 2023) **(318) 76% to 79%**. Even when a physical silence is not produced at a comma, the boundary is signaled via **syllable lengthening** to ensure the meaning is transmitted (Yiqing Zu and Xiaoxia Chen 1999) **(498)**.
*   **Predictive Planning:** Speakers explicitly adjust their breathing and pause placement to match pre-planned syntactic structures (I-Ping Wan et al. 2024/2025) **(164)**.

### **Conclusion on "Optionality"**
Reading without pauses (a ratio of 1.0) is effectively impossible for a human because of **physiological limits** (respiration) and **cognitive limits** (planning) (Brigitte Zellner 1994) **(419, 424)**. While a professional under time pressure can push the ratio toward **0.991**, a standard articulate reading by a normal speaker typically hovers around a ratio of **0.834**, where roughly 16% of the total time is dedicated to the structural pauses you described (Stockholm 2001) **(23, 26)**.
