Based on the sources, the consistency of the data is found in the **proportional scaling (ratios)** between segments rather than their absolute millisecond durations, which vary by experimental context (e.g., isolation vs. connected speech).

The following tables provide the specific ratios extracted from the research to allow for consistent application across different studies.

### **1. Consonant Geminate-to-Singleton Ratios**
Across languages, geminates are consistently found to be significantly longer than their singleton counterparts, typically following a **1.5x to 3x** scaling factor.

| Consonant Class | Ratio (Geminate : Singleton) | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General Obstruents (Stops)** | **1.5 : 1** to **3.0 : 1** | Billington |
| **General Glides (Cross-linguistic)** | **1.4 : 1** to **2.6 : 1** | Billington |
| **Lopit Glides (Total Mean)** | **1.77 : 1** | Billington |
| **Lopit Labiovelar Glide (/w/)** | **1.71 : 1** | Billington |
| **Lopit Palatal Glide (/j/)** | **1.82 : 1** | Billington |
| **Estonian Stop (Q3 : Q2 baseline)** | **~1.46 : 1** (221ms / 151ms) | Hayes |
| **Estonian Fricative (Q2 : Q1 baseline)** | **~1.34 : 1** (131ms / 98ms) | Hayes |

### **How to use this data consistently**
To resolve the conflict between the **131 ms** (Estonian fricative) and **163 ms** (Lopit glide):
1.  **Identify the Baseline:** If your target "normal" singleton fricative is **100 ms**, apply the **1.34x to 1.66x** ratio to reach a geminate value of **134–166 ms**.
2.  **Adjust for Context:** The 163 ms glide was recorded in **isolation**. Segments in isolation are inherently longer. If applying to connected speech, reduce the baseline before applying the **1.77x** ratio.
3.  **Use the 2/3 Rule for Vowels:** Regardless of the absolute duration of a vowel before a voiced consonant, the vowel before its voiceless counterpart should consistently be approximately **67%** of that duration.
